\chapter{Technology Identification \& Selection}

\section{Identification: Market Growth, Client Demand, Competition and Policy}
There are 4 signals pointed FPT toward AI. First, market growth: Gartner estimates global AI spending at about USD~1,757 billions 
in 2025, estimated to rise about USD~3,335 billions in 2027 of FPT's strategy \cite{M3}. It is close to a doubling in two years. 
Second, a shift in client demand from small pilots to company-wide deployment, which FPT describes in its large 2026 contracts 
\cite{A22}. Third, competitive pressure: AI coding tools reduce the effort. Therefore billable hours needed for 
traditional software work. Fourth, policy: both Vietnam and Japan are promoting domestic, sovereign AI capacity, and Japan 
faces an ageing population and a shortage of IT staff \cite{A15}.

\section{Selection Criteria}
Selection means deciding which of those technologies to invest in, against criteria such as strategic fit, market demand, 
technical feasibility, required investment and risk. FPT does not publish its internal report selection method, so this chapter 
applies selection criteria taught in the course to FPT's public decisions; which means it is our analysis rather than 
FPT's documented method.

\section{Selected Priorities}
Against these signals, FPT selected four priorities: GPU infrastructure (the AI Factory), an enterprise AI delivery platform 
(FleziPT), AI agents (FPT.AI), and the ability to integrate many different models.

\section{What FPT Did Not Select}
What FPT did not select is an important aspect: it does not attempt to pre-train frontier foundation models. That works may 
require billions of dollars of compute and a research workforce FPT does not have. Mistral AI, for comparison, raised EUR~1.7 billions 
in 2025 and EUR~3 billions in 2026 largely to fund model research and compute \cite{B2,B1}. FPT's strategy combines open models 
such as LLaMA and Mistral with its own platform layer \cite{A8}. FPT also keeps semiconductors as a longer-term option: it 
delivered its first commercial chip shipment, power-management ICs, to a Japanese electronics company with Restar on 30~December 2025, 
with Restar aiming to distribute up to 10~million FPT chips over 3 years \cite{M4,M5}.

Overall, FPT appears to partner for computing capacity, AI models and research,
share investment in selected ventures, buy market and domain access, and develop
its own platforms and chips.

in export controls. The sources reviewed identify no alternative GPU supplier.
The earlier low utilisation also illustrates the demand risk of installing
capacity ahead of customer contracts.

FleziPT, launched in 2025 \cite{M7}, is FPT's AI platform. It is built on
methodologies and solutions validated in real deployments and combines
human talent with a digital workforce \cite{A8}. FPT reports that the platform
contributed to an 8.2\% internal productivity increase in 2025 \cite{A24}.
Table~\ref{tab:ai-platforms} reports larger gains, such as up to 60\% faster
development; because their scope differs, these should not be read as
company-wide averages.

Flezi Metis addresses the complexity of existing software. In a deployment
for a US-headquartered automotive technology and services group, initial
pilot results on about one million lines of code showed 50\% less source
code search time and 30\% less time to complete technical tasks. FPT is
extending the platform to banking, insurance and the public sector \cite{A1}.

CASAN is a five-level AI-native capability framework that provides a
transformation roadmap \cite{A23}. It runs from early experimentation
to AI-native operations, and FPT observes that most enterprises remain
at the first two levels \cite{A25}.

In our assessment, the three tools are complementary: CASAN assesses
readiness, FleziPT supports delivery and Flezi Metis captures knowledge
held in client systems. They could turn project experience into reusable
assets and reduce reliance on billable hours, although the evidence
remains company-reported.

\chapter{Technology Protection, Governance \& Risk}
Protection means keeping control of the value a technology creates. For FPT, this has three parts: intellectual property, certified governance and regulatory compliance.

\section{Intellectual property}

FPT protects its intellectual property through the patents described in Chapter~II, through proprietary platform know-how, and through contracts with clients and partners. Because the base models belong to OpenAI, NAVER or open-source projects, FPT's patents protect only its own application layers. In practice, process know-how and client relationships are more important protections.

\section{Certified governance}

FPT's governance is evidenced by external certification:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management).} FPT Software was the first Vietnamese company to receive accredited certification for ISO/IEC 42001, the international standard for AI management systems \cite{M16,M11}.
  \item \textbf{SOC~2 and SOC~3 (cloud security).} The Vietnam AI Factory holds SOC~2 Type~II and SOC~3 reports, while the Japan site holds a SOC~2 Type~I report \cite{M17}.
  \item \textbf{HITRUST AI Security (AI security).} In February 2026, FPT's Hanoi data centre received HITRUST r2 certification, including the HITRUST AI Security Certification, which covers threats such as data poisoning, model inversion and prompt injection \cite{M18}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Regulatory compliance: Vietnam's AI Law}

Regulation is now a formal constraint. Vietnam's Law on Artificial Intelligence (No.~134/2025/QH15) was passed on 10~December 2025 with 429 of 434 votes and took effect on 1~March 2026 \cite{M19}. The law:

\begin{itemize}
  \item classifies AI systems into three risk tiers;
  \item requires conformity assessment for high-risk systems;
  \item requires important AI applications in essential sectors to run on national AI infrastructure;
  \item gives existing systems in the medical, education and financial sectors 18 months to comply \cite{M20}.
\end{itemize}

The law adds compliance costs to FPT's banking and healthcare work. At the same time, its emphasis on local infrastructure could favour domestic providers; this second point is our interpretation, not a benefit stated by FPT.

\chapter{Performance Evaluation}

\section{Investment and delivery capability}

We assess investment as moderate relative to developing a frontier foundation model. FPT builds on existing models and shares the Japanese AI Factory investment with minority shareholders Sumitomo and SBI \cite{A5,A8}. Co-investment limits the capital FPT must supply alone. In Q2 2026, FPT reported utilisation above 90\% and a profit at each of its Vietnamese and Japanese clusters \cite{A29}. This shows demand for installed capacity. One profitable quarter at two sites is insufficient to judge the efficiency of the investment over its life.

The THIO framework helps explain FPT's delivery capability. Its GPU infrastructure (technoware) complements staff expertise developed through research collaboration with Mila (humanware) \cite{A16,A13}. FleziPT captures engineering knowledge in a reusable platform (infoware), while the certified AI management system gives teams common procedures (orgaware) \cite{A22,M16}. Together these support our assessment of strong acquisitive and operative capabilities: FPT can obtain technology through partners and run it at scale. Its innovative capability is applied, as illustrated by its granted US patent for acoustic anomaly detection \cite{M13}. Reliable component ratings are unavailable, so we do not calculate a Technology Contribution Coefficient.

\section{Adoption and profitability}

In-house delivery is the main commercial route. The five-year US\$256m energy-sector contract signed in September 2025 commits FPT to deliver AI-augmented services. Its face value covers the entire engagement \cite{FptEnergy}. The six AI transformation memoranda of understanding signed in 2026, including those with UOB, SCG, SATS and ComfortDelGro, indicate prospective demand. Signing them establishes neither deployment nor recognised revenue \cite{A23}. Evidence of actual use comes from FPT.AI, which reported more than 200 enterprise clients and 10,220 active agents in 2025 \cite{A26}, and from FPT's reported deployment of Flezi Metis for an automotive technology group \cite{FptNine}.

Joint commercialisation extends this route through the Japanese venture and the collaboration with NAVER Cloud on AI Factory and GPU-as-a-Service offerings \cite{A5,A7}. The sources reviewed provide no public evidence of a separate route based on selling or licensing FPT's AI technology.

\section{Revenue contribution and strategic progress}

AI and data analytics generated VND~2,915bn in January--September 2026, already above the VND~2,612bn recorded in the whole of 2025 \cite{M1,M2}. On a year-on-year basis, revenue in this category grew by 55\%, against 12.4\% for the group after restating the prior period for FPT Telecom (Figure~\ref{fig:ai-growth}) \cite{FptNine}. The 42.6 percentage point gap shows a shift in the revenue mix from a small base. Dividing VND~2,915bn by group revenue of VND~40,023bn gives 7.3\% for the nine-month period. This measures the disclosed services category and cannot isolate AI embedded in other contracts.

Shared investment, sustained customer use and recognised revenue support a qualitative judgement that FPT's strategy is coherent and operationally on track. The available results do not disclose an operating margin or return on investment (ROI) for the AI business as a whole. Its overall financial payoff therefore remains unproven.

\chapter{Conclusion}

Gregory's five processes explain the logic of FPT's strategy. In identification and selection, AI posed a threat to billable software work while creating demand for enterprise automation. FPT chose to build solutions on existing models and acquired much of the necessary capability through collaboration \cite{A8,A13,A16}. It exploits that capability mainly through its own delivery teams, supplemented by joint commercialisation. Revenue in the reported AI and analytics category is growing rapidly but still makes up a small share of group sales \cite{FptNine}. Protection centres on what FPT develops: proprietary platforms, implementation know-how and granted patents for applications such as acoustic anomaly detection \cite{M13}. Its certified management system supports oversight, while NVIDIA GPUs and third-party models remain external technologies on which delivery depends \cite{M16,A21,A7}.

Existing customer relationships help FPT identify work worth automating. Its delivery scale lets it serve clients across markets, and its technical and industry knowledge helps teams adapt models to specific workflows. These advantages support a coherent strategy and credible operational progress. The financial payoff remains open. Future assessments should track AI operating margins and ROI, exposure to GPU and model suppliers, and AI's share of group revenue under a consistent definition.
